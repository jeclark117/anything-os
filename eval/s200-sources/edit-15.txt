Call with Client D's owner, 2026-11-19

Quick call. Notes typed up right after.

They opened by saying how glad they are that the contract is signed. They're still excited about the menu and signage redesign, and they mentioned again that the budget is the $6,500 we agreed, so nothing has changed there.

They looked through the examples of past menu work I sent. They liked the cleaner layouts best, the ones with lots of space around each dish and only a couple of typefaces. They said their current menu feels crowded and nobody can find the specials. They want the new one to be easy to read in low light, which matters in their dining room, and they want the signage to match it without looking like a copy.

Then the part that matters for scheduling. Their quiet weeks are coming earlier than usual this winter, and they'd like to use the quiet weeks to get the new menus printed and the signs up before things pick up again. So instead of the March date, they'd like us to kick off the week of February 22. They asked whether that's a problem. I checked it against everything else while we were on the call and told them yes, that works.

We talked a bit about what they'll need to send before we begin. The full current menu, including the drinks list. Photos of the dining room and the front of the building, taken in daylight. Any brand material they already have, though they admitted there isn't much. And the name of whoever will sign off on the final designs. They think it'll be the owner alone, which would keep things simple.

They asked about printing. I told them I'd recommend the printer I use, who quotes the client directly, so the printing cost doesn't run through me. They were fine with that.

Overall a friendly call. They seem organized, which helps. Follow up by email with a short list of what they need to send.
