# Motion course: week 3

general working notes

loose thoughts that apply to most motion work, mostly reminders for myself. nothing here is fixed, it's just the pile of habits I keep circling back to.

approach

- start still. if a layout doesn't hold together as a frozen frame, movement only hides the problem. get the composition feeling settled, then ask what the motion is actually for.
- motion should explain something, guide the eye, or add a feeling. if it doesn't do any of those it's decoration, and decoration wears thin fast.
- think in terms of attention. whatever moves gets looked at, so whatever moves had better deserve it. everything else should stay calm enough to let it.
- lean toward restraint. a little movement with good timing beats a lot of movement with average timing, nearly every time.
- letterforms were drawn to sit still and carry meaning, so pushing them around has a cost. worth knowing what that cost is before spending it.
- resist animating something just because the option exists. the best choice is sometimes to leave an element alone and let it be the anchor.

process

- sketch the motion on paper before opening anything. arrows, rough thumbnails, little notes about where it speeds up and where it rests. ugly is fine. the point is to find the shape of the idea cheaply.
- build a rough version with crude timing. blocky, no polish, just enough to feel the rhythm. polishing a bad rhythm is wasted effort, and it's hard to be honest about something you've already made pretty.
- work in broad strokes, then refine. big timing, then spacing, then easing, then the tiny stuff like overshoot and settle.
- watch it at full speed, then slowly, then with the sound off, then with only the sound if there's audio. each pass shows something different.
- step away for a while and come back with fresh eyes. things that felt snappy in the moment can look frantic after a break.
- show rough work to a trusted person early, before it's precious. ask what they noticed rather than whether they liked it. what they noticed tells you whether the motion is doing its job.
- keep layers named and organised even in a hurry. future me is always the person who has to open the file and figure out what past me was thinking.
- save versions generously. it's easy to lose a good accident in the middle of tweaking.
- keep a loose collection of motion that made me feel something, with a note on why. the why is the useful part, the clip itself is just a bookmark.
- when stuck, strip it back. remove effects until the idea is bare, then see what's missing. usually less is missing than expected.

things to keep in mind

- Animate type in small groups
- Keep it readable

- easing is most of the personality. linear movement feels mechanical, heavy easing feels dramatic, and the space in between is where most good choices live. match the easing to the mood of the piece, not to a default.
- contrast in speed matters more than speed itself. something fast reads as fast because of what's slow around it.
- let things rest. a moment of stillness after movement gives the eye room to land and the brain room to take it in.
- overlap and offset make motion feel alive. when everything starts and stops together it feels like a slideshow with extra steps. a slight stagger usually fixes it.
- how something enters and leaves says as much as what it does in the middle. arrivals and exits deserve attention too.
- consistency builds a visual language. if similar elements move in similar ways, viewers learn the rules without noticing and relax into the piece.
- watch for motion that fights the content. a playful bounce on a serious message, or a slow drift on something urgent, sends mixed signals.
- keep hierarchy intact while things move. what matters most should still arrive first, and secondary stuff shouldn't upstage it.
- give words time to land. a clever transition means little if the viewer is still catching up when it ends.
- remember the viewing context. people may see this small, on a dim or glaring screen, in a hurry, with distractions. what looks elegant on a big monitor can turn to mush elsewhere.
- accessibility is part of the craft. some people are sensitive to movement, and flashing or constant looping can be a real problem. a calmer alternative is worth offering where possible.
- file size and playback smoothness are design constraints too, not afterthoughts. a gorgeous piece that stutters isn't gorgeous.
- anticipation and follow-through are old tricks for a reason. a tiny wind-up before a move and a soft settle after make things feel physical, even when they're just flat shapes.

open-ended ideas

- what if the motion grew out of the structure of a word rather than out of the effects available? letter shapes have natural joints, stems, counters, terminals. let those suggest how they move.
- borrow from the physical world: ink spreading, fabric settling, paper sliding across a table. watch real behavior, then exaggerate or simplify it.
- explore motion that's nearly invisible. subtle drift, slight breathing, gentle parallax. the kind of thing people feel rather than notice.
- try motion as punctuation. commas as tiny hesitations, full stops as hard halts, question marks as a lift at the end. rhythm of speech mapped onto rhythm of movement.
- let sound lead. build the motion around a rhythm or a texture instead of adding audio afterward.
- try a version with strict limits, like only scale, or only opacity, or only position. constraints force clever solutions.
- look at how film titles, signage, and old mechanical displays handle changing text. there's a lot of craft in things that were never made with software.
- make small studies as warm-ups, sketches of motion with no client and no purpose, just curiosity. they tend to feed later work in unexpected ways.
- what would a looping piece look like if the loop point were invisible and the viewer could never say where it began?
- consider what's lost when motion is removed entirely. sometimes the strongest version is the quiet one, and it helps to know that going in.
- what if the delay between elements carried the meaning? the gap as content rather than as space to be filled.

general feeling

keep curiosity high and ego low. motion is a lot of tiny judgments stacked up, and most of them can only be made by watching, not by thinking about it. so watch a lot, and stay willing to throw away something clever if it isn't helping.
